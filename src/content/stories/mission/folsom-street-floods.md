Folsom Street between 17th and 18th streets flooded Wednesday morning, with water covering the road and both sidewalks, according to the San Francisco Chronicle. Crews closed the block at about 10:10 a.m. Before it closed, people waded to cars parked on the block and drove them out through several inches of water. A viewer video shared with ABC7 showed water rising up through the storm drains at the corner of 17th and Folsom ([ABC7](https://abc7news.com/live-updates/live-bay-area-storm-updates-tornado-warning-issued-santa-cruz-county-waterspouts-spotted-san-francisco/20045464/entry/20052180/)).

Downtown San Francisco recorded about 1.85 inches of rain by 11 a.m., close to the record for the date of 2.24 inches, set in 1918 ([CBS San Francisco](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/bay-area-central-coast-storm-november-2026-heavy-rain-strong-winds-flood-risk/)). The National Weather Service had a flood advisory in effect for the city until 11:45 a.m. and reported rain falling at close to half an inch an hour in some areas. Water also covered parts of the Embarcadero, streets in the Outer Sunset and the Cesar Chavez off-ramp from Interstate 280, and Muni rerouted the M line around flooding at 19th Avenue ([SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/11/18/atmospheric-river-drenches-sf-bringing-flood-threat-damaging-winds/)).

## Why this block floods

The blocks around 17th, 18th and Folsom were marshland until they were filled in during the 1860s and 1870s, and the fill has settled since ([Mission Local](https://missionlocal.org/2022/12/sf-floods-inundate-folsom-17th-zone/)). The area has flooded repeatedly. In December 2014, garages in the area filled with several feet of water, and water was waist-deep in one building on this block of Folsom ([Mission Local](https://missionlocal.org/2014/12/after-the-deluge-storm-updates-from-the-mission/)). On New Year's Eve 2022, when the city got 5.46 inches of rain, about three feet of water filled a garage near the corner, and a longtime resident told Mission Local it was the 35th flood he had seen there in 50 years.

The San Francisco Public Utilities Commission began building the [Folsom Area Stormwater Improvement Project](https://sfpuc.gov/construction-contracts/construction-projects/folsom-area-stormwater) in late 2023. The first phase, which upgraded about 2,855 feet of smaller sewer pipes, was finished in 2025. The main piece, a 12-foot-wide tunnel running about 4,000 feet toward Mission Bay, is still being designed, and the utility says the remaining phases will start in 2027. Its own documents give different completion dates: 2030 on the project page and 2028 in a June 2025 fact sheet. The system is designed for what the utility calls a five-year storm, about 1.3 inches in three hours, and the utility says the area will still flood in heavier rain.

Residents and businesses can pick up free sandbags at the Public Works operations yard, and the utility's Floodwater Grant reimburses property owners up to $100,000 for flood barriers and similar protection ([SFPUC](https://www.sfpuc.gov/sites/default/files/programs/grants/FWG-Fall-2025-Webinar-PPT.pdf)).

## More rain, and El Niño

The Weather Service has forecast rain through Friday, with a chance of thunderstorms Wednesday evening ([KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12109913/bay-area-braces-for-another-round-of-storms-this-week)). Before Wednesday's storm, downtown San Francisco's rain for the season was just above normal ([Golden Gate Weather Services](https://ggweather.com/seasonal_rain.htm)).

Commenters on the Chronicle's video pointed to El Niño. NOAA's November 12 update put the odds of a strong to very strong El Niño through March at better than 79% ([NOAA](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso_advisory/ensodisc.shtml)). Forecasters say that makes an active storm track like this one more likely, but they haven't tied any single storm to it. The Bay Area is at the northern edge of the area El Niño usually makes wetter, and strong El Niño winters have gone both ways here: 1982–83 and 1997–98 were very wet in California, while 2015–16 was dry ([UC San Diego](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/why_was_the_2015_16_el_nino_event_so_dry)).
