Folsom Street between 17th and 18th streets flooded Wednesday morning, with water covering the road and both sidewalks, according to the San Francisco Chronicle. Crews closed the block at about 10:10 a.m. Before it closed, people waded to cars parked on the block and drove them out through several inches of water.

https://www.instagram.com/reel/Df5J0IxqHv8/

Video by Saul Pink for the San Francisco Chronicle.

The blocks around 17th, 18th and Folsom sit in a low-lying part of the Inner Mission that has a long history of flooding in moderate to heavy storms. The San Francisco Public Utilities Commission began building the [Folsom Area Stormwater Improvement Project](https://www.sfpuc.gov/folsomstorm) in late 2023. It enlarges sewer pipes and adds a stormwater tunnel, but the utility has said the area will still be at risk of flooding in heavy rain once it's done.

Commenters on the Chronicle's video pointed to El Niño. NOAA has had an El Niño advisory in effect since June, and its November 12 update gave better than 79% odds of a strong to very strong event through March ([Fox 10](https://www.fox10tv.com/2026/11/12/el-nio-continues-strengthen-what-it-could-mean-gulf-coast-heading-into-2027/)). El Niño changes the odds of a wet winter over several months, so it can't be said to have caused any one storm.
