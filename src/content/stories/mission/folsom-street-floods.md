Folsom Street between 17th and 18th streets flooded Wednesday morning, with water covering the road and both sidewalks, according to the San Francisco Chronicle. Crews closed the block at about 10:10 a.m., after people waded to their parked cars and drove them out through several inches of water. A viewer video shared with ABC7 showed water rising up through the storm drains at the corner ([ABC7](https://abc7news.com/live-updates/live-bay-area-storm-updates-tornado-warning-issued-santa-cruz-county-waterspouts-spotted-san-francisco/20045464/entry/20052180/)).

Downtown San Francisco recorded about 1.85 inches of rain by 11 a.m., close to the record for the date of 2.24 inches, set in 1918 ([CBS San Francisco](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/bay-area-central-coast-storm-november-2026-heavy-rain-strong-winds-flood-risk/)). Water also covered parts of the Embarcadero, the Outer Sunset and the Cesar Chavez off-ramp from Interstate 280 ([SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/11/18/atmospheric-river-drenches-sf-bringing-flood-threat-damaging-winds/)).

## Why this block floods

The area around 17th and Folsom was marshland until it was filled in during the 1860s and 1870s, and the fill has settled since ([Mission Local](https://missionlocal.org/2022/12/sf-floods-inundate-folsom-17th-zone/)). It flooded in 2014 and again on New Year's Eve 2022, when a longtime resident told Mission Local it was the 35th flood he had seen there in 50 years.

The San Francisco Public Utilities Commission began its [Folsom Area Stormwater Improvement Project](https://sfpuc.gov/construction-contracts/construction-projects/folsom-area-stormwater) in late 2023 and finished the first phase of pipe upgrades in 2025. The main piece, a 12-foot-wide stormwater tunnel, is still being designed, and the utility's own documents give completion dates of both 2028 and 2030. Even then, the utility says, the area will still flood in heavy rain. Residents can get free sandbags at the Public Works operations yard.

## More rain, and El Niño

The Weather Service has forecast rain through Friday ([KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12109913/bay-area-braces-for-another-round-of-storms-this-week)). Commenters on the Chronicle's video pointed to El Niño, which NOAA gives better than 79% odds of being strong to very strong through March ([NOAA](https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso_advisory/ensodisc.shtml)). Forecasters say that makes stormy patterns like this one more likely, but they haven't tied any single storm to it, and strong El Niño winters have gone both ways here: 1997–98 was very wet in California, while 2015–16 was dry ([UC San Diego](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/why_was_the_2015_16_el_nino_event_so_dry)).
