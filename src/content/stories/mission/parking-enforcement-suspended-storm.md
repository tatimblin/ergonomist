San Francisco isn't ticketing parking meters or posted time limits from Monday, Nov. 23, through Wednesday, Nov. 25. The SFMTA made the decision ahead of heavy rain and king tides, and Mayor Daniel Lurie announced it on social media over the weekend ([Mission Local](https://missionlocal.org/2026/11/san-francisco-ticketing-suspended-sfmta-storms-floods/), [CBS San Francisco](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/san-francisco-suspends-parking-enforcement-nov-23-25-flooding/)).

In practice the break runs through Thursday. The SFMTA doesn't ticket meters, white zones or nightly street sweeping on holidays like Thanksgiving Day ([Mission Local](https://missionlocal.org/2026/11/san-francisco-ticketing-suspended-sfmta-storms-floods/)).

The point is to let people move their cars without worrying about a ticket. SFMTA spokesperson Parisa Safarzadeh told Mission Local the pause gives people "more flexibility to move their cars, check on neighbors, and prepare without the pressure of a ticket." Lurie's message to drivers: "If you are near or in a flood zone, please do not park your vehicles there starting as early as Monday morning. Find another place to park your car" ([CBS San Francisco](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/san-francisco-suspends-parking-enforcement-nov-23-25-flooding/)).

## What you can still get a ticket for

This isn't permission to park anywhere. The SFMTA is still enforcing:

- **Safety violations,** including blocked driveways and crosswalks.
- **Color curb** restrictions.
- **Street sweeping,** unless Public Works suspends sweeping. If it does, the SFMTA will stop ticketing for it too ([Mission Local](https://missionlocal.org/2026/11/san-francisco-ticketing-suspended-sfmta-storms-floods/)).

## The tides

The National Weather Service has a coastal flood warning in effect from 6 a.m. Monday through 4 p.m. Thursday ([CBS San Francisco](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/san-francisco-suspends-parking-enforcement-nov-23-25-flooding/)). The city's Department of Emergency Management says Wednesday's high tide could come close to the 1983 record at the San Francisco tide gauge. The Weather Service has forecast up to 2.7 feet of flooding above ground level in some places ([Secret San Francisco](https://secretsanfrancisco.com/sf-king-tides-flooding-free-parking/)).

Lurie has asked people to avoid travel they don't need to make, especially Monday through Wednesday. Public Works is giving out free sandbags, up to 10 per address, at its operations yard from 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. Monday through Saturday; bring an ID that shows your address. Text your ZIP code to 888-777 for city alerts ([Secret San Francisco](https://secretsanfrancisco.com/sf-king-tides-flooding-free-parking/)).
